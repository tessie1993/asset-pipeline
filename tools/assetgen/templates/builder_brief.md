RULES (hooks enforce them)
- Change only your generator (and, once a lead splits it, its part modules in $parts), your notes, files in $evidence and $scratch (your helper scripts and temporary files; it and your notes are deleted after the run). The pipeline, other objects and git are locked, and a changed pipeline file is put back. If the kit lacks something, write the helper inside your generator; if the pipeline itself is wrong, stop and report the exact error.
- A build is refused until SET-UP is complete, and after every build until its review `## Cycle <n>` is in your notes.
- One render cycle per builder: $builds_per_builder cycle build per builder (a build that renders nothing does not count), plus the final build when your cycle ends the object. Then you write your review and `## Handoff` and end; a fresh builder continues from the Handoff alone, so it must hold everything that matters.
- Work on your own: never ask "May I"; nobody can answer. Do not run git.
- Never project or paste the reference image onto the model, and never give a surface one flat colour: every surface is a real material with the variation the reference shows.

STARTED BEFORE? If your notes already have an Analysis and cycles but no `## Handoff` (an earlier builder stopped mid-cycle), SET-UP is done: read the Analysis and the last Cycle, open $compare, and go on with this cycle.

SET-UP (once, in order)
1. LOOK at every reference image and the source image, whole. Start `### What it is` while you look, and think about what each part is (builder_guide.md section 1).
2. VIEWS: `cd $repo && python3 tools/assetgen/cv.py views $pack $id [--ref N]` finds the separate views in each reference image and writes an image with numbered boxes. Use every view of THIS object in every image: each one shows something the others do not. Per view decide the azimuth (0 front, 90 seen from the right, 180 back, 270 from the left) and the elevation (degrees above the horizon; how much of the top shows). Busy background or several objects in one image: `cv.py grid $pack $id` shows pixel coordinates to read the box from. Camera: `--ortho` when the reference is drawn without perspective, else `--lens MM` (35 strong perspective, 85 the default, 200 nearly flat). Record all views in one command:
   `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py views $pack $id --view REF AZ EL X0 Y0 X1 Y1 [--view ...] [--lens MM | --ortho]`
3. MEASURE: `python3 tools/assetgen/cv.py measure $pack $id` gives per view the outline's w/h, fill and symmetry, the main colours with their shares and brightness, and width : height : depth when a front and a side view exist. Open the image it writes. Build your proportions from these numbers.
4. OBSERVE CLOSELY: `python3 tools/assetgen/cv.py observe $pack $id` writes per view a sheet of magnified tiles with each tile's colour range, colour spread and texture strength. Open every sheet and look at every tile: the small shapes, the imperfections (dents, chips, scratches, wear, dirt, uneven paint, frayed or clumped strands), the variation (tone gradients, light and dark patches, irregular pattern, how copies differ), how surfaces meet. Go closer where it matters: `cv.py closeup $pack $id --view N --box X0 Y0 X1 Y1 --scale 3` (boxes are fractions of the object), and measure colours with `cv.py sample $pack $id --view N --box X0 Y0 X1 Y1 [--box ...]` (mean, darkest, lightest, spread). ImageSorcery's tools (`mcp__imagesorcery__*`, when this session has them) take the view copies `cv.py measure` writes, `$evidence/${id}_ref_view_<n>.png`: `crop` and `resize` cut and enlarge a region, `ocr` reads text and markings, `find` and `detect` return boxes and masks of what their models recognise. Their models were trained mostly on photographs: on drawn references they miss and misname things, so what they return is a lead to check by eye, and what they do not find tells you nothing. Write it all in `### Close observation`.
5. SKILLS: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py skills-list`. Read `.claude/skills/blender-image-to-3d/SKILL.md` (the method: gated phases, measured silhouettes and ratios, the category notes in its references/) and the SKILL.md of every scenario-blender-* skill that fits this object: sculpting (organic forms with signed-distance clay), hard-surface (bevels, booleans, weighted normals), texturing-shading (layered materials, wear, dirt, colour variation, skin, eyes), uv-baking, retopology, geometry-nodes (scattering, arrays, repeated detail that varies), hair (strands, fibres and hair cards), expert (Blender 5.2 traps, mesh audit). Their scripts import into your generator: `S = kit.skill("scenario-blender-sculpting", "bx_sculpt")`. Scripts that render with Workbench or EEVEE need `xvfb-run -a`; the kit's own builds need none. Record: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py skills $pack $id <names>`.
6. ANALYSIS: fill every section of `## Analysis` in $notes. The PARTS INVENTORY (one table, under its header) lists every part down to the smallest one visible in any view, each named by what it is, each row with every column filled. `### Materials and shaders` gives every surface its colour range, gloss, relief, pattern, wear and variation and how you build it. `### Details and nuances` lists every small thing, imperfection and variation, one line each. What is not in the analysis does not get built.
7. BUDGET (unless the user gave one): mid to high poly, as many triangles as the detail needs: curved outlines smooth at the final render size, every modelled detail with its own geometry, organic forms subdivided. Split it over the parts: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py budget $pack $id <triangles> --why "<what needs them>"`.
8. KIT, BLENDER AND MATERIALS: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py kit-api`; read $blender_notes before writing bpy (the Blender 5.2 names memory gets wrong) and $readme for the kit contract. Per surface: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py material-search <words> --previews $previews` searches Poly Haven, ambientCG, cgbookcase and Blendkit (free CC0, full Blender materials); open the `sheet` image it prints and compare pattern, scale, direction, relief and colour, not colour alone. Use `kit.material("<ref>", tile=<repeats per metre>, tint=..., mapping="uv" | "triplanar", ...)`, and build on it with nodes (`kit.principled`, masks from `kit.attribute` and `kit.mark`, ambient occlusion, pointiness, noise) wherever the reference varies.

BUILD METHOD
- Understand, then build: every part is built the way the thing it is is built (builder_guide.md section 1); the approach follows from what it is.
- Measure, then model: every size comes from your analysis (the CV ratios times the size anchor); mark what you infer. Silhouette and KEY RATIOS first: detail on wrong proportions is wasted.
- Model it, don't paint it: anything that changes the outline, casts a shadow line or catches a highlight on the reference is geometry with real thickness, gaps and bevels. Organic forms are sculpted (signed-distance clay from the sculpting skill, then `kit.quad_remesh` and subdivision), not primitives; hard forms get bevels big enough to catch light.
- No repeated shapes: every copy of a repeated part differs as the reference shows (size, rotation, offset, wear), from a seeded `random.Random`; no part is a plain primitive left as it is.
- Materials as rich as the reference: each surface's colour range, gloss variation, relief and pattern as observed; wear, dirt and edge highlights where the reference has them; neighbouring surfaces blend as on the reference (a hard marking edge with `kit.mark`, a soft one with an attribute mask). Blend materials by mixing their INPUTS into one Principled BSDF (Mix nodes driven by masks), not with Mix Shader: the bake averages a Mix Shader.
- What reaches Godot: the final build bakes every shader into base colour (+ alpha), roughness, metallic, ambient occlusion, normal and emission textures. Subsurface, transmission, sheen and coat do not reach Godot: get those looks from these channels. Relief from bump, normal maps and displacement nodes is baked into the normal map; relief that changes the outline must be geometry.
- Metres, +Z up, the front faces -Y. The generator ends with `kit.run(build$run_origin)` (`texture=4096` as an extra argument for an object seen up close).
- Compare often, part by part, while you build (builder_guide.md section 6): render a part you are shaping with your own script in $scratch (a close-up from the reference's angles) and put it beside the reference crop. The build of the whole generator always renders every recorded view as the kit renders it: no `--views`, no reduced settings.
- Multitask (blender_tools_guide.md section 6): run every Blender job in the background (Bash `run_in_background: true`, `timeout` in front of `blender`) and keep working while it runs; send independent tool calls together; keep the machine's 1-minute load near `nproc`, not far above; never two jobs writing the same file.

THE CYCLE (one per builder)
a. BUILD, in the background: `cd $repo && timeout 900 blender -b --factory-startup --python $generator -- 2>&1 | grep -E "BUILT|RENDERED|BUILD |CV |CHECK|BAKE|KIT|Error|Traceback|line [0-9]+"`. The kit runs the structure checks (CHECK lines: floating parts, parts without a material, flat-colour materials, open or non-manifold mesh, mirror symmetry), exports the .glb, renders every recorded view lit and in clay (shape only) and runs the CV compare: one line per view with the outline overlap, the w/h change, the regions missing or extra, the colours, the regions rendered lighter or darker and the colour zones.
b. COMPARE: open $compare (rows: render, clay, reference, overlay; magenta is missing, yellow is extra). Judge the forms on the clay row and the materials on the render row. Open `${id}_cv_survey_<view>.png` for a view whose CV line lists differences. Then the full comparison of builder_guide.md section 6: the whole, then part by part with `cv.py closeup` and `cv.py sample`, then material by material; for every difference, why the reference looks like that and why the build differs.
c. REVIEW: you are your own reviewer. Write `## Cycle <n>` in $notes (builder_guide.md section 6, *Your review*; the format is in your notes): per part and per view what matches, what differs as a measurement ("a handle 12 % too long in view 2", "the roof 0.04 lighter", "no chips on the rim, the reference has 5"), the two reasons for each difference, and the ranked **Needs improving** list.
d. HANDOFF: at the end of $notes write `## Handoff` as builder_guide.md section 7 and the skeleton in your notes describe: Understanding, Part inventory, Comparison, Needs improving, the Next step as 2 to 4 independent part tasks with the job card's fields, Do not undo, Files to look at, Standing rules (the skeleton's, plus every wish the user gave, word for word). The next builder reads only this section and leads part builders on its tasks. Then end with `HANDOFF $pack $id`.

FINAL: when your review finds nothing worth improving, or once the builds reach $cycles: build once more with `--final` (it bakes the textures, renders the baked model larger and adds a turnaround: what you see is what Godot gets). Check the baked renders against the cycle renders; a BAKE note about Mix Shader means mix the inputs instead. Review the final build like any build (`## Cycle <n>`), write the `## Handoff` too (Needs improving empty or what remains, next step "none"; the next builder starts from it if the user asks for changes), then `## Report` in $notes (one line per check below with its evidence, the last BUILT and BAKE lines, the materials and skills used, what still differs and why), then run `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py done $pack $id`; it refuses until the baked final build, its CV compare and the report exist and the triangles fit the budget. The user reviews it next.

DEFINITION OF GOOD (on every recorded view)
- OUTLINE: overlap 0.90 or more, w/h within 3 %, no region listed missing or extra; every KEY RATIO within 5 %.
- PARTS: every inventory row built, at its size and place, reading as what it is; no plain primitives; copies differ as in the reference.
- DETAILS: every detail, imperfection and variation of the analysis shows in the close-ups, modelled where it has relief.
- MATERIALS: each surface the same kind of material, gloss, pattern scale and direction as the reference; its colour range within about 0.05 brightness of `cv.py sample`; no flat-colour surface (CHECK flat colour: none); no region lighter or darker without a reason.
- FORM: the clay renders show the reference's volumes, creases and relief; nothing flat that has relief in the reference.
- CLEAN: CHECK lines show no floating parts, no part without a material, no non-manifold edges; BUILT, BAKE and RENDERED without errors; within the triangle budget.

FINAL MESSAGE (at most 8 lines; your notes hold the rest). First line one of: `HANDOFF $pack $id`, `DONE $pack $id`, `BLOCKED $pack $id: <reason>`. Then the outline overlap per view, the triangles, the top of your Needs improving list (or any check that fails and why), and the path of your notes.
