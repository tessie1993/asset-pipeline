SET-UP (once, in order)
1. LOOK at every reference image and the source image, whole. Start `### What it is` while you look.
2. VIEWS: `cd $repo && python3 tools/assetgen/cv.py views $pack $id [--ref N]` finds the separate views in each reference image and writes an image with numbered boxes. Use every view of THIS object in every image: each one shows something the others do not. Per view decide the azimuth (0 front, 90 seen from the right, 180 back, 270 from the left) and the elevation (degrees above the horizon; how much of the top shows). Busy background or several objects in one image: `cv.py grid $pack $id` shows pixel coordinates to read the box from. Camera: `--ortho` when the reference is drawn without perspective, else `--lens MM` (35 strong perspective, 85 the default, 200 nearly flat). Record all views in one command:
   `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py views $pack $id --view REF AZ EL X0 Y0 X1 Y1 [--view ...] [--lens MM | --ortho]`
3. MEASURE: `python3 tools/assetgen/cv.py measure $pack $id` gives per view the outline's w/h, fill and symmetry, the main colours with their shares and brightness, and width : height : depth when a front and a side view exist. Open the image it writes. Build your proportions from these numbers.
4. OBSERVE CLOSELY: `python3 tools/assetgen/cv.py observe $pack $id` writes per view a sheet of magnified tiles with each tile's colour range, colour spread and texture strength. Open every sheet and look at every tile: the small shapes, the imperfections (dents, chips, scratches, wear, dirt, uneven paint, clumped or frayed strands), the variation (tone gradients, light and dark patches, irregular pattern, how copies differ), how surfaces meet. Go closer where it matters: `cv.py closeup $pack $id --view N --box X0 Y0 X1 Y1 --scale 3` (boxes are fractions of the object), and measure colours with `cv.py sample $pack $id --view N --box X0 Y0 X1 Y1 [--box ...]` (mean, darkest, lightest, spread). ImageSorcery's tools (`mcp__imagesorcery__*`, when this session has them) take the view copies `cv.py measure` writes, `$evidence/${id}_ref_view_<n>.png`: `crop` and `resize` cut and enlarge a region, `ocr` reads text and markings, `find` and `detect` return boxes and masks of what their models recognise. Their models were trained mostly on photographs: on drawn references they miss and misname things, so what they return is a lead to check by eye, and what they do not find tells you nothing. Write it all in `### Close observation`.
5. SKILLS: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py skills-list`. Read `.claude/skills/blender-image-to-3d/SKILL.md` (the method: gated phases, measured silhouettes and ratios, the category notes in its references/) and the SKILL.md of every scenario-blender-* skill that fits this object: sculpting (organic forms with signed-distance clay), hard-surface (bevels, booleans, weighted normals), texturing-shading (layered materials, wear, dirt, colour variation, eyes, skin), uv-baking, retopology, geometry-nodes (scattering, arrays, repeated detail that varies), hair (fur and hair cards), expert (Blender 5.2 traps, mesh audit). Their scripts import into your generator: `S = kit.skill("scenario-blender-sculpting", "bx_sculpt")`. Scripts that render with Workbench or EEVEE need `xvfb-run -a`; the kit's own builds need none. Record: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py skills $pack $id <names>`.
6. ANALYSIS: fill every section of `## Analysis` in $notes. The PARTS INVENTORY (one table, under its header) lists every part down to the smallest one visible in any view, each row with every column filled. `### Materials and shaders` gives every surface its colour range, gloss, relief, pattern, wear and variation and how you build it. `### Details and nuances` lists every small thing, imperfection and variation, one line each. What is not in the analysis does not get built.
7. BUDGET (unless the user gave one): mid to high poly, as many triangles as the detail needs: curved outlines smooth at the final render size, every modelled detail with its own geometry, organic forms subdivided. Split it over the parts: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py budget $pack $id <triangles> --why "<what needs them>"`.
8. KIT, BLENDER AND MATERIALS: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py kit-api`; read $blender_notes before writing bpy (the Blender 5.2 names memory gets wrong) and $readme for the kit contract. Per surface: `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py material-search <words> --previews $previews` searches Poly Haven, ambientCG, cgbookcase and Blendkit (free CC0, full Blender materials); open the `sheet` image it prints and compare pattern, scale, direction, relief and colour, not colour alone. Use `kit.material("<ref>", tile=<repeats per metre>, tint=..., mapping="uv" | "triplanar", ...)`, and build on it with nodes (`kit.principled`, masks from `kit.attribute` and `kit.mark`, ambient occlusion, pointiness, noise) wherever the reference varies.

BUILD METHOD
- Measure, then model: every size comes from your analysis (the CV ratios times the size anchor); mark what you infer. Silhouette and KEY RATIOS first: detail on wrong proportions is wasted.
- Model it, don't paint it: anything that changes the outline, casts a shadow line or catches a highlight on the reference is geometry with real thickness, gaps and bevels. Organic forms are sculpted (signed-distance clay from the sculpting skill, then `kit.quad_remesh` and subdivision), not primitives; hard forms get bevels big enough to catch light.
- No repeated shapes: every copy of a repeated part differs as the reference shows (size, rotation, offset, wear), from a seeded `random.Random`; no part is a plain primitive left as it is.
- Materials as rich as the reference: each surface's colour range, gloss variation, relief and pattern as observed; wear, dirt and edge highlights where the reference has them; neighbouring surfaces blend as on the reference (a hard marking edge with `kit.mark`, a soft one with an attribute mask). Blend materials by mixing their INPUTS into one Principled BSDF (Mix nodes driven by masks), not with Mix Shader: the bake averages a Mix Shader.
- What reaches Godot: the final build bakes every shader into base colour (+ alpha), roughness, metallic, ambient occlusion, normal and emission textures. Subsurface, transmission, sheen and coat do not reach Godot: get those looks from these channels. Relief from bump, normal maps and displacement nodes is baked into the normal map; relief that changes the outline must be geometry.
- Metres, +Z up, the front faces -Y. The generator ends with `kit.run(build$run_origin)` (`texture=4096` as an extra argument for an object seen up close).
- Fast where it does not cost quality: `--views N` renders one view while you shape a part; one Blender run at a time.

CYCLES (the standard is $cycles builds; stop sooner only when every check of the definition of good passes)
a. BUILD (Bash timeout 900000): `cd $repo && blender -b --factory-startup --python $generator -- 2>&1 | grep -E "BUILT|RENDERED|BUILD |CV |CHECK|BAKE|KIT|Error|Traceback|line [0-9]+"`. The kit runs the structure checks (CHECK lines: floating parts, parts without a material, flat-colour materials, open or non-manifold mesh, mirror symmetry), exports the .glb, renders every recorded view lit and in clay (shape only) and runs the CV compare: one line per view with the outline overlap, the w/h change, the regions missing or extra, the colours, the regions rendered lighter or darker and the colour zones.
b. LOOK: open $compare (rows: render, clay, reference, overlay; magenta is missing, yellow is extra). Judge the forms on the clay row and the materials on the render row. Open `${id}_cv_survey_<view>.png` for a view whose CV line lists differences, and use `cv.py closeup` and `cv.py sample` on the details, imperfections and material variation: the render must show what Close observation found.
c. WRITE `## Cycle <n>` in $notes: what matches (keep it), what differs as a measurement ("ears 12 % too tall", "belly patch 0.04 lighter", "no scratches on the left arm"), its cause in the model and the fix. Apply the fixes, then build again. Never break what passes to fix something else.

FINAL: when every check passes, or after $cycles builds: build once more with `--final` (it bakes the textures, renders the baked model larger and adds a turnaround: what you see is what Godot gets). Check the baked renders against the cycle renders; a BAKE note about Mix Shader means mix the inputs instead. Write `## Report` in $notes (one line per check below with its evidence, the last BUILT and BAKE lines, the materials and skills used, what still differs and why), then run `python3 tools/assetgen/pack.py done $pack $id`; it refuses until the baked final build, its CV compare and the report exist and the triangles fit the budget. A critic who did not build the object reviews it next.

DEFINITION OF GOOD (on every recorded view)
- OUTLINE: overlap 0.90 or more, w/h within 3 %, no region listed missing or extra; every KEY RATIO within 5 %.
- PARTS: every inventory row built, at its size and place; no plain primitives; copies differ as in the reference.
- DETAILS: every detail, imperfection and variation of the analysis shows in the close-ups, modelled where it has relief.
- MATERIALS: each surface the same kind of material, gloss, pattern scale and direction as the reference; its colour range within about 0.05 brightness of `cv.py sample`; no flat-colour surface (CHECK flat colour: none); no region lighter or darker without a reason.
- FORM: the clay renders show the reference's volumes, creases and relief; nothing flat that has relief in the reference.
- CLEAN: CHECK lines show no floating parts, no part without a material, no non-manifold edges; BUILT, BAKE and RENDERED without errors; within the triangle budget.

FINAL MESSAGE (at most 8 lines; your notes hold the rest). First line one of: `DONE $pack $id`, `HANDOFF $pack $id`, `BLOCKED $pack $id: <reason>`. Then the outline overlap per view, the triangles, any check that fails and why, and the path of your notes.
